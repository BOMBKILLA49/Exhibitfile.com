How it works

Four steps. No legal background needed.

1

Create a case

One case for each situation — like your custody case. Name it something your lawyer will recognize.

2

Write what you’re proving

In your own words: “They moved out on Oct 22, not earlier.” Each point gets its own evidence, in date order.

3

Add entries & receipts

Pick the day, write what happened and attach screenshots, photos, PDFs or receipts. Note what each one shows.

4

Make a binder

One tap creates an organized PDF with a timeline and numbered exhibits — to send your lawyer or print for court.

Questions

Frequently asked

Do I need to be in court already?

No. Many people start documenting when they first think they might need records someday. Notes written at the time things happen are far more convincing than memories pieced together later.

Can I add screenshots from months or years ago?

Yes. Every screenshot and photo gets its own “date this happened,” so a text from 2023 is filed under 2023 even if you add it today.

Should I make a separate case for each thing I want to prove?

No — make one case for each situation (for example, your custody case) and add each thing you want to prove as a point inside it. That way one piece of evidence can support several points, and everything ends up in one binder.

Will my lawyer be able to use the binder?

That’s what it’s designed for: a dated timeline, a section per point, numbered exhibits with your notes, and a spending summary — in a standard PDF they can read, print or share.

Who can see what I put in the app?

Only you. Everything stays on your phone unless you choose to share a binder. You can also turn on App Lock so the app needs Face ID or your passcode to open.

Is ExhibitFile legal advice?

No. ExhibitFile is a record-keeping tool. It helps you organize your information; it doesn’t replace a lawyer. Talk to an attorney about your situation, and only record calls or audio where your state’s law allows it.
